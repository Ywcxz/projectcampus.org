Project CAMPUS

Connecting All Members, Places, and University Services

A proposal for a different approach to digital transformation at Visayas State University: a digital layer over the physical university that would connect its people, places, knowledge, and services.

Read the proposal → See what it proposes ↓

By Leo M. Subingsubing, a VSU Main Campus alumnus

What it proposes

One product, the social/academic network, with a campus intranet as its long-term infrastructure.

The one product

A social/academic network: Chapter 3 of the master document

Designed so a question reaches someone who can answer, and the answer stays for the next batch.

It would be VSU's own online space for students, faculty, staff, and offices: academic at its core, with organizations, events, and campus life around it. No ads, and no feed built to hold attention.

  • Routing Questions go to the office or person who can answer.

  • Memory Confirmed answers stay, dated and findable.

  • Trust Every notice shows who stands behind it, and whether it still holds.

Long-term infrastructure

A campus intranet: Chapter 2 of the master document

Campus first, with the internet as the outer ring.

What VSU makes would live on VSU's own network and servers first. If the internet link is cut, campus services would keep working for anyone on campus Wi-Fi.

  • Devicethe latest notices open with no connection
  • CampusVSU's servers, fiber, and Wi-Fi
  • VSU-widethe other campuses and offices
  • Interneteverything from outside, still open

It builds on the fiber, Wi-Fi, and typhoon-hardened data center VSU already runs, and is offered to VSU ICT and DIGITS as a direction, not a request.

Questions

Is CAMPUS a VSU project?

No. It is an independent proposal by one alumnus, Leo M. Subingsubing, who works on it full-time with no team, partner, or institutional collaboration. It is not affiliated with Visayas State University, and no VSU office has reviewed, endorsed, approved, funded, or adopted it.

Why not just use Facebook?

Facebook is built to hold attention, and group chats to carry a conversation. Neither is built to get a question to the person who knows the answer, keep that answer for the next batch, or show who stands behind it and whether it still holds. The network is designed for those three jobs, and would work alongside the group chats people already use.

Would it replace VSU's own systems?

No. Requests, course work, grades, staff chat, and advisories would stay in VSU's own systems. The network would help people find them, and carry their notices with the source shown.

What would it ask of VSU?

The first approach will be a concept note to the Office of the Vice President for Student Affairs and Services, with two separate asks: permission to run a student survey and interviews, and interest in CAMPUS. The intranet is a direction offered to VSU ICT, not a request: CAMPUS asks VSU to build, fund, or host nothing for it.

Has the network been built or tested?

Not yet. No field research has been run, so every problem CAMPUS addresses is still a hypothesis. The first build will be a small prototype of the network, on made-up data, which needs nothing from VSU.
